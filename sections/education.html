My design philosophy

“A good interaction invites an experiment, then makes the result easy to notice.”

I design for discovery. People should be able to try something meaningful, see how the system responds, and understand why it changed. I value clear feedback, user control, and a focused interface. In Four-Part Harmony, sound and movement made team dynamics something people could test instead of only read about. Walkthroughs helped us make those relationships easier to interpret.

01 / MAKE THE INVISIBLE VISIBLE

Let behavior carry meaning.

Sound, movement, and synchronization can reveal a relationship more directly than a static description.

02 / GIVE FEEDBACK CONTEXT

Show what changed and why.

Labels and visual states should explain the result, not rely on color alone.

03 / LET PEOPLE EXPERIMENT

Give users meaningful control.

A clear action and visible consequence help people learn through trying.

04 / REDUCE BEFORE ADDING

Protect the main idea.

Supporting information should help the interaction make sense without competing with it.
