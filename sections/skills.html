01 / SKETCHING

How to sketch well

Following Buxton’s properties of sketches

  • Creation: quick, timely, inexpensive, and plentiful. Sketches are disposable, but I keep them as evidence of my process.
  • Style: a clear sketch vocabulary, distinct gesture, minimal detail, and refinement that matches the idea.
  • Function: suggest and explore rather than confirm. Ambiguity invites new interpretations.
02 / SKETCH vs PROTOTYPE

Choosing the right tool

After Buxton

Suggest → DescribeExplore → RefineQuestion → AnswerPropose → Test Provoke → ResolveTentative → SpecificEvocative → DidacticNoncommittal → Depiction

Sketches ask questions; prototypes answer them.

03 / PROCESS MODELS

Structuring a design process

  • Design Thinking: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test, implement, with loops back to earlier stages.
  • Double Diamond: understand the opportunity, define it precisely, explore alternatives, then test and narrow down (right design, then design right).
  • Frame–Diverge–Converge: frame the problem, generate many ideas, then choose.
04 / IDEATION

Generating ideas before judging them

  • Osborn’s multi-part ideation approach for diverging before converging.
  • The 10+10 method for producing many alternatives quickly.
  • Comparing options against criteria to converge on a shared concept.

HOW I WORK

I choose tools to make the next question easier to answer. Sketches help me compare directions early; prototypes let me test behavior; concise visual feedback helps people understand what changed.
