CPSC 581 gave me names and reasons for decisions I used to make by instinct. Each card covers one idea: what it is, where it shows up in my two projects, and what it changed in how I work.

A What I learned in CPSC 581

01 · Sketching

Sketch to explore, not to decide

Buxton, Sketching User Experiences (2007)

Good sketches are quick, cheap, plentiful, and a little ambiguous. That openness invites new readings instead of locking in one answer.

01
My interface and character sketches let the team compare metaphors before writing any code.
02
Three input concepts (phone wand, stylus, webcam) were sketched and compared first.

What changed: I now sketch alternatives before committing, and keep them as evidence of the process.

02 · Sketch vs prototype

Sketches ask; prototypes answer

Buxton (2007)

Suggest → DescribeExplore → RefineQuestion → AnswerPropose → TestTentative → Specific
01
Sketches asked “which metaphor?”; the prototype answered “can people read sync?”
02
The gesture-mapping sketch set the rules; six coded iterations tested them.

What changed: I pick the cheapest tool that answers the current question.

03 · Process models

Diverge before you converge

Design Council Double Diamond (2005) · Design Thinking · Frame–Diverge–Converge

Explore the problem and many solutions widely, then narrow down on purpose: first design the right thing, then design the thing right.

01
Diverge → converge → prototype → evaluate → refine.
02
Diverge → compare → prototype → test → refine → extend.

What changed: my second project added an explicit compare step and an extend step.

04 · Ideation

Generate many ideas before judging any

Osborn, Applied Imagination (1953) · Greenberg et al., 10+10 method (2012)

Defer judgement and aim for quantity, then cut down to the most promising ideas and develop variations of those.

01
Four personal concepts plus a shared room came before the band.
02
Three hardware directions came before committing to webcam pinch.

What changed: I stopped treating the first good idea as the answer.

05 · Criteria-based comparison

Decide against the brief, not taste

Course method · qualitative concept matrix

Rate each candidate against criteria taken from the requirements, so the team can see and challenge the trade-offs.

01
Four directions rated on identity and team dynamics; the band won on both.
02
Inputs rated on embodiment, teaching value, and hardware needs.

What changed: disagreements became about criteria, which are much easier to resolve.

06 · Heuristic evaluation

Check the design against known principles

Nielsen, 10 usability heuristics (1994)

A fast, structured inspection for status visibility, error prevention, recognition over recall, and minimalist design.

01
Visibility of status → consistent Waiting / Drifting / In sync states.
02
Error prevention → blocked empty demos, safe tracking loss, a mouse fallback.

What changed: I review feedback and errors before polishing visuals.
