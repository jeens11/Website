03 · Philosophy

Design for
discovery.

Values · approach
· tools I keep and drop

“A good interaction invites an experiment, then makes the result easy to notice.”

People learn a system fastest by trying something and watching it respond. So I design for that loop: a clear action, a visible consequence, and just enough explanation to understand why it happened. Four-Part Harmony let people test a team dynamic by dragging teammates together. AirSculpt took it further: the response tells you what to do next.

A Values

01 · Make the invisible visible

Let behaviour carry meaning.

Sound, movement, and sync can show a relationship faster than a paragraph can.

Seen in 01: personality becomes tempo and timing · 02: the expert’s stroke becomes a 3D ghost

02 · Feedback with context

Show what changed and why.

A number or a colour alone is ambiguous. Pair it with a label or a direction.

Seen in 01: “personality similarity” with closest/different traits · 02: blue = Pull, orange-red = Push

03 · Let people experiment

Give users real control.

A clear action with a visible result lets people learn by doing, not by reading.

Seen in 01: drag teammates closer to test sync · 02: replay the ghost and practise on your own clay

04 · Reduce before adding

Protect the main idea.

Supporting text and controls should help the interaction make sense without competing with it.

Seen in 01: detail moved into a collapsible panel · 02: floating panels keep the clay dominant

B Approach

  1. 01

    Frame

    Turn the brief into a question and the criteria I’ll judge answers by.

  2. 02

    Diverge

    Sketch many cheap options, including ones I expect to reject.

  3. 03

    Converge

    Compare against the criteria, as a team, out loud.

  4. 04

    Prototype & test

    Build the smallest thing that answers the riskiest question, then walk through it.

  5. 05

    Refine

    Fix what confused people first; polish visuals last.

C Tools & processes I adopted, and ones I set aside

Adopted

  • Low-fidelity sketchingIt’s cheap to throw away, so I explore more before committing.
  • Criteria tablesThey tie choices to the brief, not to whoever argues loudest.
  • Heuristic walkthroughsA fast check of status and feedback before user time is spent.
  • Fallback pathsAirSculpt’s mouse mode: a lesson should never depend on perfect tracking.
  • Animation + short labelsMotion draws attention; a two-word state confirms the meaning.

Set aside

  • Single global scoresOne scored untouched clay at ~97%. I now measure the specific task.
  • Extra hardwareA stylus or phone wand adds precision but shuts people out. A webcam doesn’t.
  • Auto-playing guided toursFour-Part Harmony dropped its 50-second tour. Let people try first.
  • Text-heavy panelsBehaviour first; details on demand.
  • Colour aloneEvery colour state also gets a label.
